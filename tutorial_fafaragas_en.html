Gate Pattern Carving

On this short tutorial page we first review the necessary tools and the wood material, then step by step present the carving process: from planning and sketching through driving-in the outline all the way to background removal.

Click the images to view them larger.

Tools

Metal chisel (outliner)

For every line you need to find the matching chisel, then drive the lines into the wood with a hammer, fixing the contour of the pattern.

Hammer

Used to drive the chisels during carving. Steel on steel.

Wooden mallet

A wooden striking tool for “softer” hits, specifically for wood-handled chisels. Wood on wood.

Wood-handled chisel

Mainly used for removing the background. Thanks to its design it’s easy to control and allows precise carving.

The piece: board

What to watch for

  • Make sure the wood is secured firmly (with a clamp on the table).
  • The pattern always “starts somewhere and ends somewhere” — give it a clear closure.
  • The bark edge is decorative, but while working make sure the line doesn’t “tear.”

The process

1

Designing the pattern

First we decide what we want to carve. It helps if the curve of the pattern “leads,” and it consciously starts from something and arrives into something.

tip: start with simple shapes tip: it’s faster to iterate on paper
2

Choosing the wood and sketching the pattern

We choose the board (oak/alder), then draw the pattern onto the wood. It’s worth putting down the main lines “loosely” first, then refining them.

tip: trace lightly, don’t scratch
3

“Driving in” the contour with a metal chisel

With the outliner chisel (metal chisel) and a hammer we follow the lines of the drawing. Here the goal is still fixing the contour.

tip: short, controlled strikes
4

Removing with a chisel – making the pattern stand out

After the full contour is done, we “remove” the surrounding areas, so the motif rises from the wood.

tip: remove in layers, not deep all at once
5

Motifs – the final result

When we’ve carefully removed all areas and the pattern stands out nicely, we’re done. Optionally, the carving can be painted — as was common on old Székely gates.

Tip: paint the vines green. Tip: the tulip is red.
