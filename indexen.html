Tradition in Motion

Introduction

Our project's goal is to introduce local handicraft traditions and thereby strengthen community cohesion. We aim to create an inclusive environment where anyone can participate and learn. The program helps participants acquire new skills, which can assist in career choices. In addition to preserving traditions, we consider active participation important, so it’s not only about individual development, but also about strengthening our community. We emphasize:

  • the effective promotion of traditional crafts among younger audiences
  • cultural education and the organization of events
  • the development of artistic and design skills
  • the learning of traditional craft techniques
  • the use of modern communication tools in the service of tradition preservation
Why was this project created?
To revive Transylvanian craft traditions and give young people the opportunity to actively take part in carrying them forward.
Which traditions do you aim to highlight in particular?
Among others: wood carving, weaving, felting, furniture painting, herbal knowledge, and enamel art.

December - Sharing and Reflection

December was a month of sharing and reflection. Throughout the year, we aimed to make our programs accessible to everyone, both children and adults. We feel that many young people were engaged and had a meaningful experience. Additionally, as part of the “School Differently” program, we gave local students a small taste of traditional crafts.

December also gave us the opportunity to look back and evaluate the past year from the project’s perspective. We shared our individual experiences, highlighted skills we had acquired, and reflected on how much we learned about each other, our environment, and our traditions. This inspired a desire to continue the work we started and apply the knowledge we gained.

November– Mastering

In November, we reviewed furniture painting, wood carving, weaving, and felting. This provided an opportunity for more conscious observation of details. We learned, for example, that the decoration of Székely gates differs in the regions of Csík and Udvarhely, just as the motifs of furniture painting also vary: the Almás style is different from the Kalotaszeg style. We also learned about the master woodcarvers, thanks to whom this craft is still alive today. We also found out that in many cases the host tried to make their own Székely gate, thereby further enriching this world of motifs.

October – Weaving

During October, we gained insight into the traditional world of carpet weaving. We became familiar with how the loom operates, as well as with the use of the shuttle. The activity was especially enjoyable and exciting for young children. During the program, we recycled old, worn-out clothing, thereby contributing to environmental protection and to the development of an environmentally conscious mindset.

September - Felting

In September, we immersed ourselves in the world of felting. Our shared work was accompanied by plenty of experiences, creations, and smiles. The little ones loved making felt balls, while the older kids created more exciting and complex pieces – felt figures, holders, and even small cushions.

August – Furniture Painting

During August, we held our furniture painting workshop. We would like to take this opportunity to thank the instructors and teachers for their dedicated and conscientious work. As a result of our joint efforts, a truly meaningful and beautiful workshop was realized, the value of which is clearly reflected in the finished artworks. During the session, we gained many interesting insights into the meanings of the patterns. For example, we learned that the tulip traditionally symbolizes a female figure, while the carnation represents a male. The shape of the petals can also convey additional messages, such as indicating whether a particular piece was created for an older or a younger person.

July – Sustainable and Eco-Friendly Forestry

In July, we held our workshop on sustainable and eco-friendly forestry. The rich forest along the Máréfalva stream provided an ideal location. We would like to express our gratitude to Uncle Laci, who contributed to the day’s success with his expertise and experience. We gained a wealth of exciting and useful information – for example, we learned that during forest planting, we should primarily think of our grandchildren, as the trees we plant today will bring value to their generation. We also learned that pest control is crucial: without proper protection, decades of work can be lost in just a few weeks.

June – Enamel Art Workshop at the Cultural House in Székelyudvarhely

In June, we held our enamel workshop, where everyone could unleash their creativity. We worked with small copper plates, which we first coated and then fired in a kiln. Next came the decoration phase: everyone painted their favorite motifs on the plates, which were then fired again. The process was not only exciting but also visually stunning – for instance, the bright orange enamel turned into a deep, elegant red after firing, which surprised us all. The shared creative experience was truly memorable and something we will surely talk about for a long time!

May – Székely Gate Carving

In May, we organized our gate carving workshop, where we explored various traditional techniques. During the session, we learned that the most widespread method in the area is the so-called "driving chisel" technique. The design is first drawn on the wood, then carved precisely along the lines using a driving chisel and hammer. The next step is to use the clearing chisel to remove excess wood around the pattern, thus highlighting the motifs. Interestingly, we found out that the driving chisel is usually made of iron and requires a hammer, while the clearing chisel is mostly wooden, suitable for finer work and used with a mallet – a smaller wooden hammer.

April – Project Presentation

In April, we presented our project to local youth, who received the initiative with openness and enthusiasm. During the meeting, we received many valuable ideas and suggestions from them on how to make the project more well-known and appealing to the broader community. It was inspiring to see how creatively and actively they engaged with the topic.

March – Online Space

In March, we prepared both the online and offline platforms through which we plan to showcase and promote our project in the future. We also designed the project's unique logo, aiming to harmoniously combine traditional motifs with modern visual elements – expressing the connection between past and present.

February – Project Kick-Off

In February, Tardos participated in a training session in Bucharest aimed at preparing for ESC projects. During the program, we gained many useful and valuable insights into project management. The training helped us better understand how to plan, organize, and effectively implement our own ESC project.

Personal Reflections

At the beginning of the year, my goal was to learn how traditional crafts can be brought closer to young people through modern tools and storytelling. When we presented furniture painting, felting, or Székely gate carving on social platforms, it was important to me to show not only the final result but also the experience behind the creative process.

Tradition lives when we know how to tell its story.

It was especially inspiring to see how openly young people responded to the workshops. Visual communication and authentic storytelling together can have a powerful impact. I feel we contributed to building a bridge between tradition and modern communication, which strengthened my professional interest in developing further in the field of cultural marketing.

— Nikolett

For me, this project was a real organizational challenge. The different workshops — from enamel training to weaving and gate carving — each required different preparation, tools, and scheduling. My goal was to ensure every event ran smoothly and that each participant received the necessary attention and equipment.

Creativity becomes an experience through good organization.

In practice, I experienced how important foresight and flexibility are. I learned to balance the creative process with precise organization. I feel my project management skills have developed significantly, especially in organizing cultural programs in a way that is both structured and experiential.

— Kinga

For me, the most exciting task was visually expressing the value represented by traditional crafts. When designing the logo, I consciously aimed to combine traditional motifs with modern forms. This symbolized for me the essence of the whole project: the meeting of past and present.

Tradition is inspiration, not just heritage.

During the workshops, I drew a lot of inspiration from traditional motifs — especially the rich ornamentation of furniture painting and Székely gate decorations. I learned that traditional patterns are not only relics of the past but can gain new life in contemporary design. This experience helped ground my artistic perspective in deeper cultural foundations.

— Boglárka

This year was particularly important for me because I had the opportunity to share my practical knowledge with others. Through Székely gate carving and furniture painting workshops, I experienced both the responsibility and the joy of teaching. It was important that participants not only learn techniques but also understand the meaning of the motifs and the mindset behind the craft.

Value lies not only in creation but also in sharing it.

The workshop on sustainable forestry made a particularly deep impression on me. It made me realize that wood is not just a raw material but a heritage. I feel I developed professionally in technical details, but perhaps even more importantly, I became more confident in passing on knowledge.

— Tardos

For me, this project meant experiencing cultural heritage in a living way. I had always been interested in heritage preservation theoretically, but now I saw how it becomes a community experience. During the workshops, I consistently aimed for participants not only to learn techniques but also to understand their historical and cultural context.

Community experience keeps tradition alive.

It was important that the project not become merely a nostalgic look back but remain relevant in the present. I learned that preserving cultural heritage works best through active participation and shared experiences. This year confirmed that I would like to continue participating in initiatives that build bridges between generations and eras.

— Lea
